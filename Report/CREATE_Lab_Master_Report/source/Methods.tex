\subsubsection{Training Method}
Building upon the Maniptrans paper, we use the pretrained imitator model, and train the residual model from scratch.
We also use the same techniques used in the paper, using curiculum learning, early termination and reference state initialisation (RSI). The curriculum sets the gravitational constant $G$ to zero and the friction coefficient $F$ to a high value at the start of training, so that the hands can grip objects firmly and align with the reference trajectories cheaply before having to contend with realistic physics; as training progresses $G$ is restored to its true value and $F$ is reduced to a suitable value, so that the policy ends up acting under approximately real interactions. We adopt reference state initialisation (RSI), each episode initialises the hands by randomly selecting a non-colliding near-object state from the preprocessed trajectory, and if the object's pose $p^{t}_{\hat{o}}$ deviates beyond a threshold $\epsilon_{\text{object}}$ the episode is terminated early, with $\epsilon_{\text{object}}$ progressively reduced to encourage more precise manipulation. We additionally use a contact termination condition: whenever the MoCap data indicates a firm grasp by the human hands, i.e.\ $D(j^{t}_{hf}, p^{t}_{o} \cdot o) < \xi^{t}$ where $\xi^{t}$ is the termination threshold, the contact force $C^{t}_{df}$ must be non-zero, and failure to meet this condition terminates the episode. This ensures that the policy learns how to utilize contact forces to perform manipulation.

\subsection{Experiemental Setup}

\textcolor{red}{insert image of block diagram demonstrating how data flows }

To collect data and perform teleoperation, we use two systems, the Apple Vision Pro (AVP) and the OptiTrack collecting data at 60 Hz. The Apple Vision Pro tracks the 21 hand keypoints in the cartesian space and wrist pose, and the OptiTrack captures the object pose. Since the two systems have different coordinate systems, we performed a coordinate frame transform to get the human data into the OpitTrack system using a tracker on the tip of the index finger of the right hand. By moving the hand around in the workspace of the AVP and the OpitTrack, we have a set of points and applying the Kabsch-Umeyama algorithm to map from the AVP to Optitrack.

We train the residual module $R$ with the actor-critic PPO algorithm \cite{ppo}, using a training horizon of 32 frames, a mini-batch size of 1024, and a discount factor of $\gamma = 0.99$. Optimization uses Adam \cite{adam} with an initial learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and a decay scheduler. All simulation is carried out in Isaac Gym \cite{isaacgym} with a time step of $1/60$~s. Training is performed on an HPC cluster equipped with NVIDIA V100 and A100 GPUs, while evaluation, visualisation, and teleoperation are run on a single NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU.
